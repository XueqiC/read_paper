\section{Introduction}
\label{sec:intro}

Knowledge distillation (KD)~\citep{hinton2015distilling} for large language models (LLMs)~\citep{zhao2023survey} has become an essential research direction for enhancing the accessibility and efficiency of Machine-Learning-as-a-Service (MLaaS)~\citep{cai2024llmaas,xu2024survey_kd_llms,yang2024survey_kdllm}\rev{: a small LLM learns to imitate a large teacher's outputs and can then be deployed under limited computational or financial resources}~\citep{sanh2019distilbert,jiao2020tinybert,wang2020minilm,li2021dynamickd,liang2023less}. Recent studies show that capability distillation, a specialization of knowledge distillation that focuses supervision on a target capability (e.g., instruction-following, reasoning, mathematics, or coding), can substantially improve the student’s performance on downstream tasks that primarily depend on that capability, while achieving these gains at lower serving cost than the large teacher model~\citep{magister2023teaching,shridhar2022distilling,xu2023wizardlm,yue2024distilling}.


\rev{For a specified target capability, a natural distillation strategy is to devote the available supervision budget entirely to that capability~\citep{taori2023stanford,chiang2023vicuna,zhang2024knowledgeable}. However, distillation updates also affect other capabilities~\citep{zhong2024revisiting,fang2025kddd_survey,cloud2025subliminal}, and the gains from additional target-specific supervision can diminish as training progresses. These observations motivate a budget-allocation question: \emph{can adapting the mixture of capability-targeted supervision improve the target capability more effectively than devoting the entire budget to its own data?} To ground this question, we run a controlled empirical study: we repeatedly distill the student under several budgets, each time spending the entire budget on one of eight core capabilities (General Knowledge, Reasoning, Math, Code, Tool Use, Long-Context Understanding or LCU, Steerability, and Multilinguality), and evaluate it on all of them. This yields a budget-dependent capability transfer matrix whose diagonal holds on-target gains and whose off-diagonal entries show how performance is redistributed.} Across budgets, we observe two consistent patterns: \textit{(i) capability-specific distillation induces systematic, budget-dependent transfer to other capabilities rather than isolated improvements}, and \textit{(ii) increasing the budget for a single target produces smaller additional target gains while making the average harm to non-target capabilities more pronounced}\rev{. These patterns motivate the allocation question but do not settle it: they show that the value of a token depends on where the budget has already gone, not that mixing supervision must outperform target-only distillation, which our experiments test directly.}

Building on these observed insights, we propose \textit{ReAD}, a \textit{Re}inforcement-guided c\textit{A}pability \textit{D}istillation framework for large language models \rev{(Figure~\ref{fig:overview}). ReAD first learns task-conditioned capability priorities, a simplex-constrained allocation prior over capabilities. It then spends the budget in short steps, distilling the student on teacher answers to prompts sampled from the current allocation and feeding the measured capability change to an uncertainty-aware contextual bandit, whose reward favors task-aligned gains and adds a penalty for regressions on prioritized capabilities. Online data mixing has been studied for pre-training and skill learning~\citep{chen2023skillit,albalak2023odm,chen2025aioli}; ReAD studies target-oriented allocation in teacher-supervised distillation, with rewards built from measured capability changes. Across eight target capabilities, two teacher--student pairs, and two budgets, ReAD achieves higher mean on-target scores than target-only baselines, improves over static and greedy allocation schedules with the generator held fixed, and shows smaller off-target declines than single-capability distillation.}

To sum up, this paper makes the following key contributions:
\begin{itemize}[leftmargin=*, itemsep=1pt, topsep=1pt]
    \item \textbf{\rev{Empirical observation.}}
    \rev{In our models and budgets, single-capability distillation causes budget-dependent cross-capability transfer and diminishing target gains, motivating adaptive allocation.}
    \item \textbf{\rev{Method.}}
    \rev{ReAD allocates the budget stepwise, combining a task-conditioned prior, measured capability feedback, and uncertainty-aware exploration, with a local analysis of its mechanisms.}
    \item \textbf{\rev{Experiments.}}
    \rev{ReAD achieves higher mean on-target scores on eight target-capability distillation tasks, and matched-generator comparisons and transfer diagnostics show where gains come from.}
\end{itemize}
