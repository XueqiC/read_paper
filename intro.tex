\section{Introduction}
\label{sec:intro}

Knowledge distillation (KD)~\citep{hinton2015distilling} for large language models (LLMs)~\citep{zhao2023survey} has become an essential research direction for enhancing the accessibility and efficiency of Machine-Learning-as-a-Service (MLaaS)~\citep{cai2024llmaas,xu2024survey_kd_llms,yang2024survey_kdllm}\rev{. A small LLM learns to imitate the outputs of a large teacher and can then be deployed with limited computational or financial resources}~\citep{sanh2019distilbert,jiao2020tinybert,wang2020minilm,li2021dynamickd,liang2023less}\rev{. Related work lowers serving cost in other ways, for example by routing each query to the model whose capabilities match it~\citep{cheng2026latentrouter} or by handing a multi-turn conversation to a small surrogate model distilled from the large one~\citep{cheng2026soma}}. Recent studies show that capability distillation, a specialization of knowledge distillation that focuses supervision on a target capability (e.g., instruction-following, reasoning, mathematics, or coding), can substantially improve the student’s performance on downstream tasks that primarily depend on that capability, while achieving these gains at lower serving cost than the large teacher model~\citep{magister2023teaching,shridhar2022distilling,xu2023wizardlm,yue2024distilling}.


\rev{For a specified target capability, a natural distillation strategy is to devote the available supervision budget entirely to that capability~\citep{taori2023stanford,chiang2023vicuna,zhang2024knowledgeable}. However, distillation updates also affect other capabilities~\citep{zhong2024revisiting,fang2025kddd_survey,cloud2025subliminal}, and the gains from additional target-specific supervision can diminish as training progresses. These observations raise a budget-allocation question. \emph{Can adapting the mixture of capability-targeted supervision improve the target capability more effectively than devoting the entire budget to its own data?} To study it, we run a controlled experiment. We repeatedly distill the student under several budgets, each time spending the whole budget on one of eight core capabilities (General Knowledge, Reasoning, Math, Code, Tool Use, Long-Context Understanding or LCU, Steerability, and Multilinguality), and evaluate it on all eight. The result is a budget-dependent capability transfer matrix. Its diagonal holds on-target gains, and its off-diagonal entries show how performance moves across capabilities.} Across budgets, we observe two consistent patterns: \textit{(i) capability-specific distillation induces systematic, budget-dependent transfer to other capabilities rather than isolated improvements}, and \textit{(ii) increasing the budget for a single target produces smaller additional target gains while making the average harm to non-target capabilities more pronounced}\rev{. These patterns suggest that the value of an extra token may depend on where earlier tokens went. They leave open whether mixing supervision helps the target, and our experiments examine that question.}

Building on these observed insights, we propose \textit{ReAD}, a \textit{Re}inforcement-guided c\textit{A}pability \textit{D}istillation framework for large language models \rev{(Figure~\ref{fig:overview}). ReAD sets task-conditioned capability priorities, spends the budget in short steps, and lets an uncertainty-aware contextual bandit choose the mix of capability-targeted supervision at each step from the measured change in the student. Online mixing has been explored for pre-training and skill learning~\citep{chen2023skillit,albalak2023odm,chen2025aioli}; ReAD brings it to teacher-guided distillation for one target.}

\rev{The main contributions of this paper are as follows.}
\begin{itemize}[leftmargin=*, itemsep=1pt, topsep=1pt]
    \item \textbf{\rev{Budget-resolved transfer.}}
    \rev{We measure how distilling each of eight capabilities changes all eight at three budgets. Transfer is signed and budget-dependent, and the target's marginal gain shrinks as its budget grows (Figure~\ref{fig:capability_transfer_heatmaps}). Spending a whole budget on the target can therefore leave part of it underused, which makes the allocation itself worth optimizing.}
    \item \textbf{\rev{Target-oriented allocation for distillation.}}
    \rev{ReAD learns capability priorities from low-budget probe interventions, generates teacher supervision on the fly, and reallocates the budget step by step with an uncertainty-aware bandit. Its reward comes from measured capability changes and penalizes regressions on prioritized capabilities, so the allocation follows the student's current state.}
    \item \textbf{\rev{Gains under a fixed budget.}}
    \rev{ReAD has the highest mean on-target score for every target, budget, and model pair we test, e.g., $+3.8$ and $+5.6$ points on Steerability over the strongest baseline at $20$M and $150$M. With the generator held fixed, it beats the best static and greedy schedules by $1.0$--$1.4$ points, and its off-target decline at $150$M is about a quarter of single-capability KD's.}
\end{itemize}
