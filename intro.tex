\section{Introduction}
\label{sec:intro}

Knowledge distillation (KD)~\citep{hinton2015distilling} for large language models (LLMs)~\citep{zhao2023survey} has become an essential research direction for enhancing the accessibility and efficiency of Machine-Learning-as-a-Service (MLaaS)~\citep{cai2024llmaas,xu2024survey_kd_llms,yang2024survey_kdllm}\rev{: a small LLM learns to imitate a large teacher's outputs and can then be deployed under limited computational or financial resources}~\citep{sanh2019distilbert,jiao2020tinybert,wang2020minilm,li2021dynamickd,liang2023less}. Recent studies show that capability distillation, a specialization of knowledge distillation that focuses supervision on a target capability (e.g., instruction-following, reasoning, mathematics, or coding), can substantially improve the student’s performance on downstream tasks that primarily depend on that capability, while achieving these gains at lower serving cost than the large teacher model~\citep{magister2023teaching,shridhar2022distilling,xu2023wizardlm,yue2024distilling}.


Despite rapid progress in capability distillation, most existing methods still treat capabilities as if they can be improved independently~\citep{taori2023stanford, chiang2023vicuna,zhang2024knowledgeable}, even though empirical evidence suggests that optimizing one capability often triggers broad, unintended shifts in the model’s overall capability profile~\citep{zhong2024revisiting, fang2025kddd_survey,cloud2025subliminal}. \rev{This mismatch matters most under a fixed token budget, where downstream utility depends on the targeted capability and on the other capabilities the task implicitly requires. Ignoring cross-capability interactions then wastes tokens in two ways: the extra gain on the target shrinks as its budget grows, and the same updates can degrade non-target capabilities the task needs. We call tokens spent on such updates \emph{budget waste}. To examine this gap, we run a controlled empirical study: we repeatedly distill the student under several budgets, each time spending the entire budget on one of eight core capabilities (General Knowledge, Reasoning, Math, Code, Tool Use, Long-Context Understanding or LCU, Steerability, and Multilinguality), and evaluate it on all of them. This yields a budget-dependent capability transfer matrix whose diagonal holds on-target gains and whose off-diagonal entries show how performance is redistributed.} Across budgets, we observe two consistent patterns: \textit{(i) capability-specific distillation induces systematic, budget-dependent transfer to other capabilities rather than isolated improvements}, and \textit{(ii) increasing the budget for a single target produces smaller additional target gains while making the average harm to non-target capabilities more pronounced}, which together explain why naively scaling the budget for one capability can be inefficient\rev{ and why the allocation itself should be optimized}.

Building on these observed insights, we propose \textit{ReAD}, a \textit{Re}inforcement-guided c\textit{A}pability \textit{D}istillation framework for large language models \rev{(Figure~\ref{fig:overview}). It then spends the budget in short steps: at each step it samples capability-labeled prompts according to the current allocation, queries the teacher, distills the student on the answers with a standard loss, and feeds the resulting capability-profile change to an uncertainty-aware contextual bandit, whose reward favors task-aligned gains and penalizes spillover and token cost. To sum up, this paper makes the following key contributions:
\begin{itemize}[leftmargin=*, itemsep=1pt, topsep=1pt]
    \item \textbf{Empirical study of cross-capability interactions.}
    \rev{Distilling toward a single capability systematically changes the others, an interaction that existing methods ignore.}
    \item \textbf{Budgeted capability distillation formulation.}
    \rev{We cast capability distillation as allocating a fixed budget across interacting capabilities, trading utility against side effects.}
    \item \textbf{ReAD: reinforcement-guided capability distillation.}
    \rev{ReAD infers task-relevant capabilities, generates targeted data with controlled difficulty, and allocates the budget with an uncertainty-aware contextual bandit that adapts as the student changes.}
    \item \textbf{Theory analysis of the capability interdependence.}
    \rev{A local analysis explains cross-capability transfer and diminishing returns, and why uncertainty-aware allocation helps.}
\end{itemize}
