\section{Methodology}
\label{sec:method}

  \centering

  $\mathbf{r}_\tau$, fixed per task. \textbf{(2)} From the context $\mathbf{x}_t$

ReAD treats Problem~\ref{prob:budget_alloc} as state-dependent budget allocation
over capabilities \rev{(Figure~\ref{fig:overview})}. \rev{It first sets task-conditioned capability priorities; then, at each step, it} generates
capability-targeted teacher supervision\rev{ and} uses an uncertainty-aware contextual
bandit to choose the next token allocation. As the student profile changes, ReAD
can shift budget away from saturated or high-spillover capabilities.
\rev{ReAD is \emph{off-policy}~\citep{agarwal2024gkd}: the student learns from
teacher completions, and on-policy losses~\citep{gu2024minillm,ko2024distillm}
could replace this objective without changing the allocator.}

\subsection{\rev{Task-Conditioned Capability Priorities}}
\label{sec:method:essential}

\paragraph{\rev{Capability-priority vector.}}
\rev{For each downstream task $\tau$, ReAD builds a task card
$\mathcal{D}^{\mathrm{spec}}_\tau$ from non-test data (a short description,
input/output format, evaluation target, and a few exemplars) and estimates from
it a capability-priority vector $\mathbf{r}_\tau\in\Delta_{|\mathcal{C}|}$, a
simplex-constrained allocation prior in which $r_{\tau,c}$ is the priority of
capability $c$ for the task utility $U_\tau(\cdot)$. High-mass capabilities
are prioritized in the candidate allocations, and regressions on them are penalized. It lives on the simplex because token allocations cannot be
negative; negative sensitivities enter through signed capability changes and
the regression penalty of Section~\ref{sec:method:bandit}.}

\paragraph{\rev{Priority identifier and its supervision.}}
\rev{A lightweight identifier $g_\phi$ (a small Transformer encoder over the
task card, a two-layer MLP, and a softmax head) maps the card to
$\mathbf{r}_\tau=g_\phi(\mathcal{D}^{\mathrm{spec}}_\tau)$. Since
$\mathbf{r}_\tau$ is not observed, we supervise it through low-budget
interventions in capability space. Writing $U_\tau(S)=F_\tau(\mathbf{s}(S))$
for the measured capability profile $\mathbf{s}(S)\in\mathbb{R}^{|\mathcal{C}|}$,
a small intervention that moves the profile by $\Delta\mathbf{s}$ changes the
utility by}
\begin{equation}
\Delta U_\tau
=
U_\tau(S')-U_\tau(S)
\approx
\nabla_{\mathbf{s}}F_\tau(\mathbf{s})^\top\Delta\mathbf{s}
\label{eq:local_linear}
\end{equation}
\rev{(first-order expansion, Appendix~\ref{sec:app_method}). This expansion
motivates the training signal only: $\mathbf{r}_\tau$ is not an estimate of
the true sensitivity but an allocation prior whose inner product with a
capability change is fit to the observed utility change, as follows.}

\paragraph{Offline pretraining.}
\rev{We build an intervention set
$\mathcal{Z}=\{(\mathcal{D}^{\mathrm{spec}}_\tau,\Delta\mathbf{s}^{(m)}_\tau,\Delta U^{(m)}_\tau)\}_{\tau,m}$
from auxiliary tasks and non-test splits: for each task we sample sparse
allocation vectors $\mathbf{w}^{(m)}\in\Delta_{|\mathcal{C}|}$ (a random
support with Dirichlet weights), run a short probe distillation with a small
budget $b_{\mathrm{probe}}$ to obtain an intervened student $S^{(m)}$, and
record $\Delta U^{(m)}_\tau=U_\tau(S^{(m)})-U_\tau(S_0)$ and
$\Delta\mathbf{s}^{(m)}_\tau=\mathbf{s}(S^{(m)})-\mathbf{s}(S_0)$. The
identifier is pretrained to predict the utility change from the capability
change,}
\begin{equation}
\min_\phi
\sum_{(\tau,m)}
\Big(\Delta U^{(m)}_\tau
- g_\phi(\mathcal{D}^{\mathrm{spec}}_\tau)^\top\Delta\mathbf{s}^{(m)}_\tau\Big)^2
+\lambda_{\mathrm{ent}}\,\mathcal{H}\!\left(g_\phi(\mathcal{D}^{\mathrm{spec}}_\tau)\right),
\label{eq:req_pretrain}
\end{equation}
\rev{where the entropy penalty discourages near-uniform priority vectors.
At deployment, $\mathbf{r}_\tau$ is computed once per task and reused for the
whole run; because the identifier is trained once and shared across tasks, it
is not charged to the per-run distillation budget.}

\subsection{On-the-Fly Capability-Targeted Data Generation}
\label{sec:method-generator}

\rev{Given $\mathbf{r}_\tau$, ReAD instantiates prompts on the fly from a
capability-labeled template library, where each template specifies an
instruction scaffold, typed slots filled from deterministic seeds, and
output-format constraints. A deterministic difficulty score $d_c(x)\in[0,1]$
averages capability-specific control factors (e.g., reasoning hops, required
functions and tests, or schema rules; Eq.~\ref{eq:difficulty_score} in
Appendix~\ref{sec:app_method}) and splits prompts into easy, medium, and hard
tertiles. At step $t$, ReAD samples capabilities according to $\mathbf{w}_t$,
queries the teacher $T$ for a completion $y$, and immediately distills on
$(x,y)$. A curriculum moves the within-capability mix from easy toward hard
examples without affecting the allocation, and per-template caps keep any single
scaffold from dominating the generated data.}

\subsection{Contextual Bandit for Capability Allocation}
\label{sec:method:bandit}

\paragraph{Decision loop.}
ReAD runs for $T_{\mathrm{step}}=20$ decision steps\rev{, with the bandit update, data resampling, and proxy refresh at every step (Section~\ref{subsec:exp_setup})}. At step $t$, the context summarizes the \rev{task priorities}, current student profile, remaining budget, and recent allocation history:
\begin{equation}
\mathbf{x}_t=[\mathbf{r}_\tau;\mathbf{s}^{\mathrm{probe}}(S_t);b_t;\boldsymbol{\rho}_t],
\label{eq:bandit_context}
\end{equation}
where $\boldsymbol{\rho}_t$ stores a short record of recent allocations and observed gains. The profile $\mathbf{s}^{\mathrm{probe}}(S_t)$ is computed with a small fixed monitoring suite, while full held-out benchmark evaluation is used only for final reporting and coarse calibration.

\paragraph{Candidate allocations and distillation.}
At each step, ReAD selects an allocation $\mathbf{w}_t$ from a finite candidate set $\mathcal{A}(\tau)$ consisting of local perturbations around the previous allocation and sparse actions concentrated on the top-$k$ capabilities under $\mathbf{r}_\tau$. Weights are chosen from a fixed grid and renormalized to sum to one. Given $\mathbf{w}_t$, ReAD samples capability-targeted data and updates the student by minimizing $\mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{distill}}(\theta_t)
=-\mathbb{E}_{(x,y)}\sum_{j=1}^{|y|}
\log p_{\theta_t}(y_j\mid x,y_{<j})$ to produce $S_{t+1}$.

\paragraph{Proxy reward.}
After the update, ReAD measures the probe-profile change
$\Delta\mathbf{s}^{\mathrm{probe}}_t=\mathbf{s}^{\mathrm{probe}}(S_{t+1})-\mathbf{s}^{\mathrm{probe}}(S_t)$ and forms
\begin{equation}
\widehat{R}_t
=\mathbf{r}_\tau^\top\Delta\mathbf{s}^{\mathrm{probe}}_t
-\beta\,\mathrm{Spill}_t
-\lambda\,\mathrm{cost}_t, \quad\ \mathrm{Spill}_t=
\sum_{c\in\mathcal{C}_{\mathrm{pri}}}
 r_{\tau,c}\,[-\Delta s^{\mathrm{probe}}_{t,c}]_+
\label{eq:reward}
\end{equation}
Here $\mathcal{C}_{\mathrm{pri}}$ contains the top-$k$ \rev{(prioritized)} capabilities under $\mathbf{r}_\tau$, and $\mathrm{cost}_t$ is the token budget consumed at the step. The first term rewards task-aligned capability gains\rev{ and the second adds an extra penalty for regressions on prioritized capabilities, which already lower the first term. Because every step in our experiments consumes the same number of tokens, the third term is constant; the budget is enforced by the fixed total $B$ and the update schedule.}

\paragraph{Reward model and UCB allocation.}
\rev{We append $(\mathbf{x}_t,\mathbf{w}_t,\widehat{R}_t)$ to a history buffer
and train an ensemble of $J$ two-layer MLP reward regressors
$\{h_{\eta_j}\}_{j=1}^J$, each on a bootstrap resample of the 10\% profiling
split plus the logged transitions ($3.2$k--$4.9$k examples per task). For a
candidate $\mathbf{w}$, $\mu(\mathbf{x}_t,\mathbf{w})$ and
$\sigma(\mathbf{x}_t,\mathbf{w})$ denote the ensemble mean and standard
deviation (Appendix~\ref{sec:app_method}), and ReAD selects the next allocation
with an upper-confidence rule,}
\begin{equation}
\mathbf{w}_{t+1}
=\arg\max_{\mathbf{w}\in\mathcal{A}(\tau)}
\mu(\mathbf{x}_{t+1},\mathbf{w})+\kappa\,\sigma(\mathbf{x}_{t+1},\mathbf{w}),
\label{eq:ucb}
\end{equation}
with $b_{t+1}=b_t-\mathrm{cost}_t$. At coarse development checkpoints, we fit an affine calibration from cumulative proxy reward to observed utility change and reduce exploration if the development utility stagnates. Unlike static mixture-regression approaches such as RegMix~\citep{liu2024regmix}, ReAD repeatedly re-estimates the best next allocation from the current student state and remaining budget.
