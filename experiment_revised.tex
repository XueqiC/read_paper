\section{Experimental Setting}
\label{subsec:exp_setup}

\textbf{Models and budgets.}
We mainly distill Llama-3.3-70B-Instruct into Llama-3.1-8B-Instruct, and report
Qwen2.5-72B-Instruct to Qwen2.5-14B-Instruct results in
Appendix~\ref{sec:app_experiment}. All methods use the same student checkpoint,
training recipe, and $20$M/$150$M distillation-token budgets.

\textbf{Evaluation protocol.}
We evaluate eight capabilities using Appendix
Table~\ref{tab:capabilities-benchmarks}. For each benchmark, 10\% of non-test
data is used for profiling, \rev{priority} inference, and allocation updates; the
held-out split is reserved for final reporting. Generation uses temperature $0$
with the same prompt template across methods. We report mean $\pm$ standard
deviation over three seeds.

\textbf{Baselines.}
For each capability, we build a held-out-filtered distillation pool from its
benchmark prompts. For multi-dataset capabilities, we use balanced dataset-level
sampling. The six main baselines combine three teacher signals---final response
(Resp), chain-of-thought plus response (CoT), and token-level logits (Logit)---
with two objectives, SFT and KD. Each baseline is one-hot at the capability
level, spending the full budget on the target-capability pool.
\rev{We also compare against four allocation baselines that share ReAD's
generator, teacher, and budget and differ only in how $\mathbf{w}_t$ is chosen (Figure~\ref{fig:diagnostics}c). They are \emph{Uniform static}
($\mathbf{w}_t=\mathbf{1}/|\mathcal{C}|$), \emph{Task-static mix}
($\mathbf{w}_t=\mathbf{r}_\tau$), \emph{Greedy one-step} (re-selects
$\mathbf{w}_t$ each step from the same context, including the remaining budget,
but without the uncertainty bonus, $\kappa=0$), and
\emph{Grid-searched} (best fixed allocation in $\mathcal{A}(\tau)$ on the
profiling split). These baselines separate the allocation schedule
from the rest of the pipeline. Finally, we adapt three published capability-specific distillation methods to our teacher--student pair and budget, namely step-by-step distillation~\citep{hsieh2023stepbystep} for reasoning,
Equation-of-Thought Distillation (EoTD)~\citep{zhu2024distilling} for math,
and CodePLAN~\citep{sun2024enhancing} for code.}

\textbf{ReAD implementation.}
ReAD runs for $20$ decision steps, with bandit updates, proxy refreshes, and data
resampling every $1.0$M tokens in the $20$M setting and every $7.5$M tokens in
the $150$M setting. Proxy refresh adds 3.12\% wall-clock overhead, the full online loop adds
5.63\%, and the reusable \rev{priority} identifier costs 3.79 GPU-hours to train
once.
\rev{\textbf{Cost accounting.} $B$ counts the teacher-generated tokens the
student is trained on and is identical for all methods. Two ReAD-specific costs fall outside $B$, and we report them separately. The identifier is trained once from auxiliary tasks and reused for every task and budget, so its 3.79 GPU-hours are amortized. The fixed probe suite accounts for the 3.12\% overhead above.}

\section{Experiments}
\label{sec:exp}

target-capability distillation tasks under the same budget}; \textbf{RQ2} \rev{whether
the allocation schedule matters when the generator is held fixed}; \textbf{RQ3}
\rev{budget efficiency and the trade-off between target gain and spillover}; and
\textbf{RQ4} transfer beyond capability-aligned benchmarks\rev{. Figure~\ref{fig:signif}
gives a statistical view of every comparison.}

\subsection{RQ1: \rev{On-Target Performance under a Fixed Budget}}
\label{subsec:exp_results}
\label{subsec:rq1}

\rev{Table~\ref{tab:llama_20m} reports on-target performance across separate distillation tasks. For each target capability, every baseline is distilled toward it alone, ReAD allocates supervision across capabilities for the same target, and all methods are scored on that target. ReAD has the highest mean on-target score for all eight targets at both budgets.} \rev{The
largest margins are on Steerability at both budgets, followed by Code and Tool
Use at $20$M and by Multilingual and Code at $150$M.} \rev{The baselines also differ from ReAD in how supervision is generated, so this comparison reflects the whole method. RQ2 compares allocation schedules under the same generator.} The Qwen results in Appendix~\ref{sec:app_experiment} show the same
ordering, suggesting that the improvement is not tied to a single
teacher--student pair. \rev{With three seeds, all sixteen differences are positive, and all but Reasoning at $150$M are significant before correction. After Holm correction over the eight capabilities, only Steerability at both budgets and Multilingual at $150$M remain significant (Figure~\ref{fig:signif}).}

\rev{\textbf{Margin and teacher--student headroom.} The margin tends to grow with the teacher--student headroom (Spearman $\rho=0.74$ at $150$M, $p=0.04$). It is largest on Steerability and Multilingual, where the teacher leads by over $20$ points, and smallest on Reasoning ($1.8$ points). ReAD's gain over the student is $1.9\times$ that of the strongest baseline at $20$M and $1.3\times$ at $150$M, which suggests that the advantage is largest when tokens are scarce.}

\rev{Figure~\ref{fig:radar} compares ReAD with three capability-specific methods. These methods concentrate gains on their own target, as Section~\ref{sec:emp_res} predicts, and ReAD scores at least as high on each.}

\subsection{RQ2: \rev{Allocation Schedules under a Matched Generator}}
\label{subsec:rq2}

Figure~\ref{fig:diagnostics}c compares ReAD with \rev{the four} allocation
baselines \rev{of Section~\ref{subsec:exp_setup}, which share its generator,
teacher, and token budget and differ only in how $\mathbf{w}_t$ is chosen}.
\rev{Static schedules keep one allocation for the whole run. Greedy one-step re-selects it every step from the same context, including the remaining budget, but has no uncertainty bonus.} ReAD outperforms \rev{all four}, with larger margins at $150$M. \rev{The margins ($+1.0$/$+1.2$ over
Greedy one-step and $+1.2$/$+1.4$ over Grid-searched at $20$M/$150$M; seed std
$0.28$--$0.42$) are smaller than those over the one-hot baselines, probably because every schedule already mixes capabilities. Greedy one-step sits only $0.15$--$0.20$ points above the best static schedule. In this comparison, most of ReAD's gain therefore appears to come from uncertainty-aware selection added on top of dynamic updates. All four gains remain significant after Holm correction at $150$M, and so does the gain over Uniform static at $20$M (Figure~\ref{fig:signif}).}

\rev{\textbf{Component ablation.} Figure~\ref{fig:rq3_ablation} changes one component at a time. \emph{W/o Identifier} replaces the task-guided allocation with a uniform one, so it alters more than $g_\phi$ alone. \emph{W/o Adaptation} fixes the allocation, and \emph{W/o Interaction} removes the extra regression penalty. The first two variants lose the most, and removing the regression penalty costs somewhat less. Because Figure~\ref{fig:rq3_ablation} averages over all eight capabilities, it mixes on-target and off-target effects.}

Figure~\ref{fig:rq2_budget} reports budget-scaling curves for Reasoning, Math,
Tool Use, and LCU. ReAD achieves higher performance at \rev{every} budget\rev{
and scales most steeply on Math and LCU. On Reasoning and Tool Use, the baselines narrow the gap by $150$M as all methods approach the teacher.}
\rev{ReAD also gains the most per additional $10$M tokens on six of the eight
capabilities (Appendix Table~\ref{tab:scaling}). Table~\ref{tab:equiv_budget} turns the two measured budgets into rough estimates. To match ReAD's $20$M score, the strongest one-hot baseline would need about $1.3$--$6.5\times$ as many tokens (median about $2\times$) under log-linear interpolation and $1.8$--$7.0\times$ (median about $3\times$) under linear interpolation. It does not reach ReAD's $20$M Tool Use score even at $150$M. These estimates assume that each baseline changes smoothly between the two measured budgets.}

Figure~\ref{fig:diagnostics}a--b shows \rev{the trade-off between target gain and
spillover at $150$M}. Single-capability KD achieves strong target gains but
causes larger off-target degradation, while static mixing reduces spillover at
the cost of weaker target improvement. \rev{ReAD matches the on-target gain of single-capability KD, and its off-target decline and negative-transfer rate are the lowest in the comparison.}

\subsection{RQ4: Transfer beyond Aligned Benchmarks}
\label{subsec:rq4}

\rev{On held-out XSTest~\citep{rottger2024xstest}, we reuse the pipeline without a new capability head. ReAD lowers safe-refusal and raises unsafe-refusal scores over the best SFT/KD baseline at both budgets (Figure~\ref{fig:xstest}, Appendix Table~\ref{tab:xstest}). The four differences agree in direction. None survives Holm correction (Figure~\ref{fig:signif}), so this is preliminary evidence of transfer beyond the eight capabilities.}
